A recipe tells you when to put the lid on the pot. It rarely tells you where to put the lid when you take it off.

At that moment the underside is wet with condensed steam. The handle may be hot. The counter is crowded with the cutting board, the bowl of chopped onions, the spoon you meant to use next. You hold the lid above the pot for a few seconds, letting water drip back in, while you look for a place it can safely land.

Nothing has gone wrong with the recipe. The food is doing exactly what it should. The problem is that an object has changed jobs. A lid that was useful *on* the pot is briefly in the way *off* it.

I used to think of an uncluttered work surface as a luxury: nice to have if the kitchen is large enough. But a patch of empty counter can be part of the equipment. It lets you put down what you are holding before you have decided what to pick up next. Without it, every move acquires an extra condition. To stir the pot, first move the board. To move the board, first find somewhere for the knife.

You can prepare for this with a plate or a trivet beside the stove. That is not elaborate organization. It is giving the lid a destination before it becomes urgent to find one.

Recipes are generally written in the order that things happen. A kitchen also has to accommodate the things that happen *between* steps: the hot lid, the used spoon, the empty bowl that is not quite ready for the sink. Those objects need somewhere to be while they are no longer useful for the last task and not yet dealt with by the next one.

The plate beside the stove does not improve the stew. It makes it possible to uncover the stew without having to solve a small, wet puzzle first.
